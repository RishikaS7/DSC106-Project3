## Will You Still Know Winter?

Our last chart uses the same data as mid-latitude winter means where there is one dot per winter, but asks a different question. Instead of how much warmer winters get, it asks whether the winters later in life will feel like the ones they grew up with. You choose your birth year and then the vis compares your childhood winters with the ones you’d live through at 60 to 79. The chart shades the range of winter temperatures from your childhood (ages 5–14). Every other winter is colored by how it compares. Red means warmer than any winter of your childhood, and gray means it would feel familiar. A toggle switches between cutting emissions and continuing to burn fossil fuels. Hovering a dot shows how old you’d be that winter. For example, someone born in 2005, the prediction for cutting emissions means only 2 of 20 winters later in life feel unfamiliar, while it would predict all 20 if we keep burning fossil fuels. Cutting emissions won’t bring back the winters older generations remember, but it’ll keep today’s winters more familiar.
